## In brief

- **TEE-shielded LLMs:** Unilateral weight obfuscation is found to be insecure, with attacks recovering model functionality.
- **Reverse engineering with LLMs:** LLMs are evaluated on reconstructing protobuf schemas for iOS location services, showing they are unreliable analysts but useful toolmakers.
- **LLM agent failures:** Human interventions are used as labels to localize and steer computations behind LLM agent failures.

## BiShield-TEE: On the (In-)Security of Unilateral Weight Obfuscation in On-Device TEE-Shielded LLM Partition

- Analyzes the security of unilateral weight obfuscation in TEE-shielded LLM partition.
- Demonstrates that attacks on GroupCover and ArrowCloak can elevate attack accuracy to the black-box baseline, effectively recovering model functionality.
- Proposes BiShield-TEE, which confines attack accuracy to the black-box level.
- Notes that the parameter reconstruction stage of the attack is query-free, relying solely on obfuscated weights.

- Zhiyuan Wang, Yuezhi Zhou, Ziqi Zhang, Zhu Zhang, Zhixing Tan, Yongheng Deng. "BiShield-TEE: On the (In-)Security of Unilateral Weight Obfuscation in On-Device TEE-Shielded LLM Partition." arXiv — https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21565

## Decoding What iOS Location Services Send to Apple: An LLM Is an Unreliable Analyst but a Useful Toolmaker

- Evaluates using frontier models (Claude Opus 4.8, Codex GPT Sol 5.6) to reconstruct schemas of protobufs sent by iPhones to Apple's location services.
- Uses rooted phones and a man-in-the-middle attack to intercept and decrypt HTTPS traffic.
- Finds that the great majority of the schemas generated by LLMs contain substantial errors that are hard to detect.
- Observes that in 413 disagreements in which the LLM gives a type, Extractor is found to be correct.

- Douglas Leith. "Decoding What iOS Location Services Send to Apple: An LLM Is an Unreliable Analyst but a Useful Toolmaker." arXiv — https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21597

## Human Interventions as Labels: Localizing, Steering, and (Sometimes) Editing the Computations Behind LLM Agent Failures

- Treats human intervention in misbehaving LLM agents as a measurement pointing at the internal computation that failed.
- Studies a controlled agent environment where the agent asks a clarifying question when the request was already unambiguous.
- Finds that the models studied ask a question when the context already answered it on 82–93% of unambiguous requests.
- Discovers that the site's location of the failure varies widely across network depth (55–81%), but its existence and the sharpness of its onset do not.

- Cesare Zavattari, Alessandro Tommasi, Giuseppe Prencipe. "Human Interventions as Labels: Localizing, Steering, and (Sometimes) Editing the Computations Behind LLM Agent Failures." arXiv — https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21708

## Learning to Steer LLM Agents and Multi-Agent Systems

- Proposes a steering agent that supervises execution on the fly and steers LLM-based multi-agent systems back on track when goal drift occurs.
- Addresses the problem of systems deviating from the overall goal during long-horizon tool use and inter-agent collaboration.
- Shows that the steering interface can achieve different steering granularities depending on the observation and action spaces.

- Leyang Shen, Yaxin Gong, Yang Zhang, Chenxiao Fan, Jiakai Wang, Dong Wang, Yang Liu, Fuli Feng, Chun Kai Ling, Tat-Seng Chua. "Learning to Steer LLM Agents and Multi-Agent Systems." arXiv — https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22316

## Forensic Analysis of the HackRF One with PortaPack Mayhem Firmware

- Establishes at source-code level where forensically relevant data reside on the microSD card of a HackRF One running Mayhem v2.0.1.
- Notes that a single piece of hardware can work as both a receiver and a transmitter over a wide frequency range.
- Identifies that unallocated clusters are not a directory but the area that the file system marks as free and in which deleted data may be found.

- Duzgun Kucuk. "Forensic Analysis of the HackRF One with PortaPack Mayhem Firmware." arXiv — https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20647
